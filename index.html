What we do

We advise leaders of complex institutions on strategy, innovation, resilience, and renewal when the forces of change redraw the map beneath their feet.

We believe every leader—and every institution—needs both a map and a compass.

A map works in stable, well-understood terrain, where the path can be planned and execution is what matters. A compass works when the terrain is changing, the questions are ill-defined, and direction must come from purpose and values rather than a plan. The mistake is not choosing one over the other. It is using the wrong one: a map in uncharted territory, or a compass where a precise plan would serve better.

A map depends on landmarks. In fog at sea, in a blizzard, in a forest with no horizon, in a desert where the dunes erase the trail, the markers disappear and you can no longer tell where you are on the map. The map becomes useless. The compass does not. It needs no landmarks, only a destination and the discipline to hold a steady heading. Leadership in uncharted terrain works the same way: when the familiar markers vanish, direction has to come from purpose and values.

We help leaders tell the two situations apart and build the capacity to work in both.

Who we are

JMO

Julio M. Ottino

Co-Founding Chair

Complexity, Systems Thinking & Institutional Transformation

Julio M. Ottino is a scientist, engineer, artist, educator, and institutional leader whose work bridges complexity science, strategic thinking, and organizational transformation. His career centers on how complex systems behave, how new ideas emerge, and how institutions can adapt and thrive amid change.

A pioneer in chaos and complexity science, Julio connects ideas across disciplines and turns abstract concepts into practical frameworks for leadership and institutional change, pairing scientific rigor with an appreciation for the human, organizational, and historical dimensions of complex challenges.

He founded the Northwestern Institute on Complex Systems (NICO), then served 18 years as Dean of Northwestern’s McCormick School of Engineering, transforming its approach to education, research, and innovation and fostering interdisciplinary initiatives in design, entrepreneurship, sustainability, and artificial intelligence.

Julio has advised Unilever, Dow, and AkzoNobel, and spoken at Accenture, Boeing, Google, and 3M, convinced that the most consequential challenges defy disciplinary boundaries and conventional management. A Guggenheim Fellow, he is a member of the National Academy of Engineering, the National Academy of Sciences, and the American Academy of Arts and Sciences.

At Two Norths Meridian, Julio brings conceptual depth and proven institutional leadership. A thinker drawn to the principles behind how systems function and evolve, he is equally adept at turning insight into enduring institutional change. He helps leaders tell when established knowledge offers a reliable map and when uncertainty demands a compass grounded in purpose, principles, and judgment.

AL

Alicia Löffler

Co-Founder

Institution-Building, Innovation & Strategic Execution

Alicia Löffler is a scientist, entrepreneur, and institutional leader whose career has focused on building organizations and innovation ecosystems that translate ambitious ideas into lasting impact. As Northwestern University’s Associate Provost for Innovation and Entrepreneurship, she founded and led the Innovation and New Ventures Office (INVO), establishing venture funds, incubators, accelerators, and strategic partnerships that helped launch more than 90 startups and generated approximately $1.8 billion in net income since 2010.

Throughout her career, she has founded and led university centers and initiatives advancing translational research, technology commercialization, and entrepreneurship. In 2024, she co-founded the Northwestern Innovation Institute at the Kellogg School of Management, which examines how innovation emerges and develops predictive approaches to accelerate scientific and technological breakthroughs.

She is also Co-Founder and CEO of ENCUE, a biotechnology company developing therapeutics for neurological disorders, and has held leadership and advisory positions across the biotechnology, investment, and innovation sectors.

Her work brings together scientific rigor, entrepreneurial vision, and institutional leadership, with a particular focus on building the structures, partnerships, and strategies that move discoveries from the laboratory into meaningful real-world impact.

Bertrand Ottino-Löffler

Co-Founder and Consultant

Mathematical Modeling, Complexity & Quantitative Analysis

Bertrand Ottino-Löffler is a mathematician, physicist, and educator whose research explores how complex systems evolve, how order emerges and breaks down, and how seemingly unpredictable behavior arises from underlying mathematical principles. A passionate educator, he brings complex mathematical and scientific concepts to life. At Two Norths Meridian, he combines theoretical rigor, computational modeling, and a talent for making complexity understandable, helping leaders challenge assumptions, recognize emerging patterns, and navigate uncertainty.
